This section reports the two evaluation deployments of
\ours{}: a static paired panel that isolates the
verification core, and a stateful online deployment that
adds the persistent rule state inside a live browser
harness.

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:exp:setting}

The task basis is inherited from our companion
benchmark \bench, which pairs 140 executable web
tasks---47 business, 35 environment, 19 health, 39
public-statistics, in 21 mechanism families---with a
faithful chart and a misleading chart whose
presentation violates an encoding convention while the
data remain drawable.\footnote{Companion
submission under review; instance construction,
taxonomy, and scoring are defined there.}
Model inputs never contain gold labels, mechanism
labels, the other chart variant, or scoring feedback.

\emph{Static panel.}
For every task the agent sees the benchmark's
misleading chart---scored against its frozen original
label---plus the public goal and four options, and must select; no navigation occurs.
Six configurations share identical inputs and decoding (temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20,
fixed seed, thinking disabled): Direct and five READ--VERIFY--DECIDE configurations with frozen prompts, one
verification round ($L=1$), and three requests per task drawing on the frozen per-task pool of one to three $O/B$ records pre-generated from the chart and public task (275 across the panel).
V3 verifies $O$ and $B$ in distinct typed passes, V4 fixes explicit numeric references, V5 adds
same-series query-point constraints with scale, time-window, and policy checks, V6 checks necessary conditions itemwise, and V7 keeps V4's
prompts with an abstention-aware decision repair
phase. The five variants trade verification content against interface strictness: what is checked versus how answers are forced. The six configurations and prompts were frozen before the rerun and seed rounds, never modified after seeing results.
Interface failures and empty selections remain in the
140 denominator; \emph{robust accuracy} throughout is
the fraction of tasks whose selection matches the
frozen original label, a single full-denominator quantity.
Frozen prompts, typed schemas, decision logs, the
interface contract, and scoring code are in the supplementary material, so every number outside the three-seed replication can be recomputed from released artifacts once its license permits release.

\emph{Online stateful deployment.}
Twelve tasks spanning all four domains, in fixed identifier order, run as live browser sessions under a
closed deployment: each task's two chart variants
execute under both ordinary and \ours{} arms, whose hooks, rule state, and re-derivation loops
run inside the harness with the actor driving a (Playwright-controlled) browser.
The submission interface is a strict typed protocol
(\texttt{typed\_scalars\_v2}; at most eight actor
calls, one re-verification round, temperature 0): a
unit ends as a scored submission, an abstention with a reason, or a failure.
Three actor systems are used---Qwen3.8-27B under
strict response formats (Batch A), the same model
behind a schema-guided proxy mirroring the sealed
structural rules (Batch B), and a frontier model, \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}, through an
OpenAI-compatible gateway (Batch C, plus one stability
rerun).

The 27B columns of Table~\ref{tab:static-main} report the single-run rerun that keeps model, seeds, and prompts frozen: direct selection drops from 81 to 73 of 140 under the identical configuration, quantifying agentic decoding's sampling variance; comparisons must stay within-run.
Within-run, V5 gains $+17$ paired decisions
($p=.0013$; 95\% CI $[+0.061, +0.231]$), V4, V6, and
V7 gain $+13$, $+13$, $+16$ ($p\leq0.0025$ each), and
V3 returns to $+2$ despite strongly directional
wrong--target flips (30 against 6, $p<.001$; caption's discordant-pair test) because its interface
failures absorb the difference.
The rerun's paired transfers are one-directional (57 keep, 33 error-to-target, 14 target-to-error, 2 target-to-empty); interface failures concentrate in V3. No configuration can abstain (every unit must select), and the same directionality holds in every seed of the matched-configuration replication, whose per-seed nets are reported in the baselines paragraph.

\paragraph{Baselines and controls.}
The clean-chart control quantifies misleading presentations' execution-level damage: direct selection
rises from 56.2\% on misleading charts to 82.1\% on the
paired clean charts (paired net $+33$, $p<.001$, CI $[+0.16, +0.27]$).
The two generic baselines do not close that gap: SC3
reaches 53.6\%, indistinguishable from direct selection
(paired net $-3$, $p=.375$)---consistent with
Section~\ref{sec:method:setting}, the object of
correction is the interpretation rule, not the answer
distribution; and the tabular-answering baseline abstains on 55 units with 18 transcription
failures, selecting with 59.7\% precision (40/67), a
paired net of $-3$ ($p=.51$).
Across the three seeds, direct selection lands at
$56.2\,\pm\,1.1$ and \ours{} at $65.7\,\pm\,0.7$ points; the per-seed paired nets $+12$, $+18$,
$+15$ pool to $+45$ decisions over 420 paired units
(exact McNemar $p=1.9\times10^{-5}$, 95\% CI
$[+0.058, +0.150]$).

\paragraph{Cross-model transfer.}
On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, direct selection drops to
46.4\% and the picture splits
(Table~\ref{tab:static-main}, 8B columns): V7 gains
$+15$ ($p=.021$; CI $[+.017,+.210]$), V5 gains $+10$ ($p=.044$), V4 and V6 remain within noise, and
V3 collapses through 51 interface failures despite
directional wrong--target flips (Appendix~\ref{app:transfer}). The panel's exact tests are unadjusted for multiplicity; on the 8B columns we read V4--V7's consistent directionality, not single-test significance. The separated verification prompts that produce
directional flips on the 27B model overwhelm the 8B
actor, so interface robustness is a first-class
variable, and prompt content and interface contract
must be evaluated jointly, not as one ``method'' factor.

\subsection{Online Stateful Results}\label{sec:exp:online}



Because the deployment's submission protocol is
fail-closed, the primary online metric is the
\emph{wrong-submission rate}---the defense-side
counterpart of agent abstention
\citep{liu2026agentabstain, luo2026agentic}: across
the four batches,
ordinary execution produced six wrong submissions, all
under the 27B actor on misleading charts, while \ours{} produced none in any batch or variant (0 of 96
defense-arm units); Table~\ref{tab:online-four} gives the full four-batch decomposition. On clean charts the defense arm pays the mirror cost---6 and 5 correct submissions against the ordinary arm's 10 (Batch C and its rerun)---the price of fail-closed verification.
Aggregate accuracy moves oppositely: ordinary execution
loses 0--5 decision points on misleading charts
against its own clean-chart performance, and the
defense arm converts correct ordinary decisions into
recorded abstentions rather than corrections.
The paired effect is strictly one-directional in both
frontier-model batches (10 and 13 preserved decisions
become abstentions; the reverse never occurs; sign tests $p=.002$ and $p<.001$), with submission precision 100\% (26/26 and 27/27).
A pilot (6 tasks, \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}) showed the same
qualitative pattern (5 correct ordinary selections
against 1 for \ours{}).


\paragraph{Interface decomposition.}
Structural and semantic failure modes separate by actor system: under the strict interface
without guidance (Batch A) 20 of 24 defense-arm units
die in engineering failures, while the schema-guided proxy (Batch B) zeroes the ordinary actor's structural failures but cuts defense-arm engineering
failures only modestly, and the direct gateway (Batch
C) leaves honest abstention as the remaining mass
(Appendix~\ref{app:interface}).

A stability rerun of Batch C produces no wrong submission in either run: 37 of 48 units stay on the same side of the submission boundary, the rest move among abstentions, engineering failures, and call-limit terminations, and completion throughput varies (Appendix~\ref{app:stability}).
