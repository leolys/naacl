This section reports the two evaluation deployments of
\ours{}: a static paired panel that isolates the
verification core, and a stateful online deployment that
adds the persistent rule state inside a live browser
harness.

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:exp:setting}

The task basis is inherited from our companion
benchmark \bench, which pairs 140 executable web
tasks---47 business, 35 environment, 19 health, 39
public-statistics, in 21 mechanism families---with a
faithful chart and a misleading chart whose
presentation violates an encoding convention while the
underlying data remain drawable.\footnote{Companion
submission under review; instance construction,
taxonomy, and scoring are defined there.}
Model inputs never contain gold labels, mechanism
labels, the other chart variant, or scoring feedback.

\emph{Static panel.}
For every task the agent sees the benchmark's
misleading chart---scored against its frozen original
label---plus the public goal and four options, and must
submit a selection; no browser navigation occurs.
Six configurations are compared on identical inputs and
decoding (temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20,
fixed seed, thinking disabled): direct selection and
five READ--VERIFY--DECIDE configurations
(Section~\ref{sec:method}) with frozen prompts and
three requests per task, whose candidate chains are the
frozen per-task pool of one to three pre-generated
$O/B$ records (275 across the panel).
V3 verifies $O$ and $B$ in distinct schema-typed
passes, V4 fixes explicit numeric references, V5
(full) adds same-series query-point constraints with
scale, time-window, and policy-boundary checks, V6
checks each necessary condition of $B$ item by item,
and V7 keeps V4's prompts with an abstention-aware
decision repair phase; full prompts are in the
supplementary material.
Interface failures and empty selections remain in the
140 denominator; \emph{robust accuracy} throughout is
the fraction of tasks whose selection matches the
frozen original label, the same full-denominator
quantity for every configuration.
The supplementary material contains the frozen prompts,
typed output schemas, per-request decision logs with
seeds and usage, the interface contract, and the
scoring code, so every number in this section can be
recomputed from the released artifacts once the
companion benchmark's data license permits public
release.

\emph{Online stateful deployment.}
Twelve tasks spanning all four domains, taken in fixed
identifier order before execution, run as live browser
sessions under a closed deployment: each task's two
chart variants execute under both ordinary execution
and the \ours{} arm, whose hooks, rule state, and
re-derivation loops run inside the harness with the
actor driving a real browser.
The submission interface is a strict typed protocol
(\texttt{typed\_scalars\_v2}; at most eight actor
calls, one re-verification round, temperature 0), in
which a unit ends as a scored submission, an abstention
with a recorded reason, or a failure.
Three actor systems are used: the 27B model under
strict response formats (Batch A), the same model
behind a schema-guided proxy mirroring the sealed
structural rules (Batch B), and the frontier model of
the pilot reached directly through the gateway
(Batch C, the formal result, plus one stability
rerun).

Table~\ref{tab:static-main} reports the original round
under the 27B open-weight VLM: direct selection sits at
57.9\% (81/140), and the full configuration V5 reaches
68.6\% (96/140), a net gain of 15 decision points
(10.7 points of accuracy) with no increase in abstention
or interface failure.
Because the panel is entirely single-decision-point,
that gain cannot be attributed to information gained
after selection: verification changes the decision that
was already available, which is the quantity the
formulation of Section~\ref{sec:method:setting} isolates.

Table~\ref{tab:static-seed} reports a matched-configuration
replication designed to separate sampling variance from
configuration effects: direct selection and the full
configuration V5 are each re-executed under three request
seeds (12345, 22345, 32345) on a single-GPU deployment
whose engine configuration matches the frozen service
except for batch admission, so all comparisons are
internal to that deployment (Appendix~\ref{app:details}
records the protocol).
The same run supplies three baselines at no additional
engine cost: a self-consistency vote SC3 over the three
direct-selection seeds \citep{wang2022selfconsistency}
(majority among non-null selections, ties recorded as
abstentions), a tabular-answering baseline in the style
of Tonglet et al. \citep{tonglet2025protecting}
(bounded transcription of the comparison-relevant chart
rows, then a decision taken from the table alone with
the image withheld), and a clean-chart control that
runs direct selection on the paired clean charts.

The 27B columns of Table~\ref{tab:static-main} report the
single-run replication that keeps model, seeds, and
prompts frozen: direct selection drops from 81 to 73 of
140 under an identical configuration, quantifying the
sampling variance of agentic decoding itself, so every
comparison must be made within the same run.
Within-run, V5 gains $+17$ paired decisions
($p=0.0013$; 95\% CI $[+0.061, +0.231]$), V4, V6, and
V7 gain $+13$, $+13$, $+16$ ($p\leq0.0025$ each), and
V3 returns to $+2$ despite strongly directional
wrong--target flips (30 against 6, $p<0.001$) because
its interface failures absorb the difference---the
separation between decision quality and interface
robustness that Section~\ref{sec:exp:online} examines
in deployment.
Paired transfer counts are one-directional in aggregate
(57 keep, 33 error-to-target, 11 target-to-error, 5
target-to-empty, 0 empty-to-target), and the panel
remains abstention-free outside V3's interface
failures.

\paragraph{Baselines and controls.}
Three observations bound what the verification gain can
be attributed to.
First, the clean-chart control: direct selection rises
from 56.4\% on misleading charts to 82.1\% on the paired
clean charts (115/140; paired net $+33$ against its own
misleading-chart run, $p<0.001$, exact CI
$[+0.16, +0.27]$), which quantifies the execution-level
damage the misleading presentations cause and the room
any defense has to recover.
Second, the majority-vote baseline: SC3 reaches 53.6\%
(75/140), statistically indistinguishable from direct
selection (paired net $-3$, $p=0.375$); aggregating
diverse decoding paths neither recovers the
misleading-chart losses nor approaches the verification
gain, consistent with the argument of
Section~\ref{sec:method:setting} that the object of
correction is the interpretation rule, not the answer
distribution.
Third, the tabular-answering baseline abstains on 55 of
140 units (its bounded table does not carry the
task-relevant evidence) and records 18 transcription
interface failures; on the 67 units where it selects,
submission precision is 59.7\% (40/67), and its paired
net against direct selection is $-3$ ($p=0.51$).
Generic mitigation therefore neither transfers the
tabular-answering gains reported for chart QA to
execution tasks nor exploits the within-chart evidence
that \ours{} uses; the seed-level variance of the two
arms is reported with Table~\ref{tab:static-seed} once
the three-seed replication completes (mean $\pm$ sd
over seeds for both arms, pooled McNemar across
seeds).

\paragraph{Cross-model transfer.}
On the 8B open-weight VLM, direct selection drops to
46.4\% and the picture splits
(Table~\ref{tab:static-main}, 8B columns): V7 gains $+15$
($p=0.021$; CI $[+0.017, +0.210]$), V5 gains $+10$
($p=0.044$), V4 $+12$ and V6 $+6$ remain within noise,
and V3 collapses---51 of 140 runs end in interface
failures of the schema-typed protocol, against 8 and 6
in the other rounds, for a net of $-15$ despite
directional wrong--target flips (18 against 10).
The interaction is instructive: the same separated
verification prompts that produce directional
wrong--target flips on the 27B model overwhelm the 8B
actor.
Interface robustness is thus a first-class variable,
and prompt content and interface contract must be
evaluated jointly rather than as one ``method''
factor.

\paragraph{Where the gains concentrate.}
Stratified by mechanism family, V5's net gains
concentrate where misleading power comes from scope and
reference manipulation: the subset-extrapolation family
(22 tasks) yields $+18$ and $+11$ across the two
rounds, while non-linear-axis and dual-axis families
are neutral to $-6$.
By domain, business carries the largest share ($+16$
and $+4$), public statistics is neutral then positive
($0$ and $+9$), health is $+6$ in both, and the
environment domain is the only negative one in both
rounds ($-5$, $-9$).
The development subset contributes one decision point
to each headline gain, and costs remain bounded: 8.84M
and 8.14M tokens across the replication rounds
(2{,}230 and 2{,}184 requests) against 8.86M in the
original round, a $2.6\times$ request overhead over
direct selection with frozen prompts and no parameter
updates.

A pilot (6 tasks, frontier model) already showed the
qualitative pattern (5 correct ordinary selections
against 1 for \ours{}).
Because the deployment's submission protocol is
fail-closed, the primary online metric is the
\emph{wrong-submission rate}: across the four batches,
ordinary execution produced six wrong submissions, all
under the 27B actor on misleading charts, while the
\ours{} arm produced none in any batch or chart variant
(0 of 96 defense-arm units).
Aggregate accuracy moves oppositely: ordinary execution
loses 3--5 decision points on misleading charts
relative to its own clean-chart performance, and the
defense arm converts correct ordinary decisions into
recorded abstentions rather than corrections.
The paired task-level effect is strictly
one-directional in both frontier-model batches (10 and
13 decisions preserved by the ordinary arm are turned
into abstentions; the reverse never occurs; exact sign
tests $p=0.002$ and $p<0.001$), and submission
precision is 100\% for both systems there (26/26 and
27/27).

\paragraph{Interface decomposition.}
The three actor systems separate structural from
semantic failure modes.
Under the strict interface without guidance (Batch A),
22 of 24 defense-arm units die in engineering failures,
and the ordinary arm ends in two call-limit exhaustions
on misleading charts.
A schema-guided proxy mirroring the sealed structural
rules (Batch B) drives the ordinary actor's structural
failures to zero (12/12 on clean charts) but cuts
defense-arm engineering failures only modestly (21 of
24): the binding constraint is semantic, since exact
public-leaf values, bounded summaries, and the
question--response count coupling cannot be expressed
by any response format---components added for
self-correction become the exposed surface, as
\citet{wu2024dissecting} analyze for compound systems.
Running the frontier model directly (Batch C) removes
the structural interface almost entirely (defense-arm
engineering failures 4 of 24), and the remaining mass
is honest abstention---12
\texttt{unresolved\_public\_evidence} verdicts, mostly
on misleading charts, where verification cannot close
the gap between the printed chart and the question's
required scope within the re-verification
budget.

\paragraph{Stability.}
Re-running Batch C under the identical configuration
changes 11 of 48 unit outcomes, every one a flip
between a correct submission and an abstention
(agreement 37/48); no wrong submission appears in
either run, so the ``never submit a wrong answer''
property is stable while completion throughput is not.
Token budgets are comparable across the two runs
(1.80M/129K versus 1.81M/115K input/output tokens,
322/242 versus 331/254 attempts/browser operations),
confirming that the stateful machinery operates within
a fixed per-unit allowance comparable to the static
panel's per-task cost.
