This section reports the two evaluation deployments of
\ours{}: a static paired panel that isolates the
verification core, and a stateful online deployment that
adds the persistent rule state inside a live browser
harness.

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:exp:setting}

The task basis is inherited from our companion
benchmark \bench, which pairs 140 executable web
tasks---47 business, 35 environment, 19 health, 39
public-statistics, in 21 mechanism families---with a
faithful chart and a misleading chart whose
presentation violates an encoding convention while the
underlying data remain drawable.\footnote{Companion
submission under review; instance construction,
taxonomy, and scoring are defined there.}
Model inputs never contain gold labels, mechanism
labels, the other chart variant, or scoring feedback.

\emph{Static panel.}
For every task the agent sees the original chart of the
benchmark---the misleading instance of the pair, scored
against its frozen original label---plus the public goal
and four options, and must submit a selection; no
browser navigation occurs.
Six configurations are compared on identical inputs and
decoding (temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20,
fixed seed, thinking disabled): direct selection and
five verification configurations implementing the
READ--VERIFY--DECIDE pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:method}
with frozen prompts, three requests per task, and a
fixed pool of 275 pre-generated observation/rule
candidates.
They differ in verification emphasis: V3 verifies $O$
and $B$ in distinct schema-typed passes; V4 fixes
explicit numeric references, dates, ranges, and
baselines; V5 (full) constrains query points with
same-series references, checks scales, time windows,
and policy boundaries, and prioritizes the
discriminating scope; V6 checks each necessary condition
of $B$ item by item, requiring positive evidence for
routine defaults; V7 keeps V4's prompts and reorients
the decision phase toward same-series position and
numeric ranges to avoid abstention on missing exact
printed values.
Interface failures and empty selections remain in the
140 denominator.

\emph{Online stateful deployment.}
Twelve tasks spanning all four domains
(six business, three environment, two health, one
public-statistics) run as live browser sessions under a
closed deployment: for each task, both chart variants
(official misleading chart, clean chart) execute under
both systems---ordinary execution without defense, and
the \ours{} arm whose hooks, rule state, and
re-derivation loops run inside the harness with the
actor driving a real browser.
The submission interface is a strict typed protocol
(interface \texttt{typed\_scalars\_v2}, at most eight
actor calls, one re-verification round, temperature~0),
in which a unit ends as a submitted action scored by the
original evaluation, an abstention with a recorded
reason, or a failure.
Three actor systems are used: a local 27B open-weight
VLM served with strict response-format constraints
(Batch A), the same model behind a schema-guided proxy
that mirrors the sealed structural rules of the protocol
(Batch B), and the frontier model of the pilot accessed
directly through the same gateway (Batch C, the formal
result, plus one stability rerun under identical
configuration).

Table~\ref{tab:static-main} reports the original round
under the 27B open-weight VLM: direct selection sits at
57.9\% (81/140), and the full configuration V5 reaches
68.6\% (96/140), a net gain of 15 decision points
(10.7 points of accuracy) with no increase in abstention
or interface failure.
Because the panel is entirely single-decision-point,
that gain cannot be attributed to information gained
after selection: verification changes the decision that
was already available, which is the quantity the
formulation of Section~\ref{sec:method:setting} isolates.

The replication run keeps the model, seeds, and prompts
frozen and re-executes the whole pipeline
(Table~\ref{tab:static-repl}, left).
Direct selection drops from 81 to 73 of 140
(57.9\% to 52.1\%) under an identical configuration,
which quantifies the sampling variance of agentic
decoding itself; every comparison must therefore be
made within the same run.
Within-run, V5 gains $+17$ paired decisions
(McNemar $p=0.0025$; exact 95\% CI on the difference in
accuracy $[+0.029, +0.214]$), and V4, V6, and V7 gain
$+13$, $+13$, and $+16$ with $p<0.005$ each, while V3
returns to $+2$.
Paired transfer counts are one-directional in aggregate
(57 keep, 33 error-to-target, 11 target-to-error, 5
target-to-empty, 0 empty-to-target), and the panel
remains abstention-free outside V3's interface
failures.

\paragraph{Cross-model transfer.}
On the 8B open-weight VLM, direct selection drops to
46.4\% and the picture splits
(Table~\ref{tab:static-repl}, right).
V7 gains $+15$ ($p=0.043$; CI $[+0.007, +0.207]$),
V4 and V5 gain $+12$ and $+10$ without reaching
significance, and V6 $+6$ is within noise.
V3 collapses: 51 of 140 runs end in interface failures
of the schema-typed protocol, against 8 in the 27B
replication and 6 in the original round, and its net
effect is $-15$.
The interaction is instructive: on the 27B model V3's
separated $O$/$B$ verification produced the largest
error-to-target correction count of any configuration
(30 against 6 reverse, $p=0.0001$), for a net of $+2$
after interface failures; the same prompts overwhelm
the 8B actor.
Interface robustness is thus a first-class experimental
variable, and prompt content and interface contract
must be evaluated jointly rather than as a single
``method'' factor.

\paragraph{Where the gains concentrate.}
Stratified by mechanism family, V5's net gains
concentrate in families whose misleading power comes
from scope and reference manipulation: the
subset-extrapolation family (22 tasks) yields $+18$ in
the 27B replication and $+11$ in the 8B transfer,
while the non-linear-axis family is neutral in the
replication and $-6$ in the 8B round, and the
dual-axis family $-5$ and $0$.
By domain, business carries the largest share in both
rounds ($+16$ and $+4$), public statistics is neutral
in the replication and positive in the 8B round
($0$ and $+9$), health is $+6$ in both, and the
environment domain is the only negative one in both
rounds ($-5$ and $-9$); the original round shows the
same qualitative pattern.
The development subset contributes only one decision
point to each headline gain ($+17$ comprises 1
development and 16 remaining tasks; $+10$ comprises 1
and 9), and all 24 development tasks score above the
run's direct-selection mean in every verification
configuration.
Costs remain bounded: the replication rounds spend
8.84M total tokens for the 27B round (2{,}230
requests) and 8.14M for the 8B round (2{,}184
requests), against 8.86M for the original round, a
roughly $2.6\times$ request overhead over direct
selection with frozen prompts and no parameter updates.

A pilot (6 tasks, frontier model, 12 units) already
showed the qualitative pattern: the ordinary arm
submitted 5 correct selections against 1 for the
\ours{} arm.
The formal batch (Table~\ref{tab:online-four}, top)
confirms it at larger scale and adds the important
control: the ordinary arm loses 3--5 decision points on
misleading charts relative to its own clean-chart
performance (8 vs.\ 10 here; 7 vs.\ 10 and 7 vs.\ 12 in
the two earlier 27B batches, which additionally contain
3 wrong submissions caused by the misleading chart per
batch), whereas the \ours{} arm submits only correct
actions and converts the rest into recorded
abstentions---7 misleading-chart abstentions, one
re-verification budget exhaustion, and two engineering
failures.
The paired task-level effect is therefore strictly
one-directional in both C batches (ordinary
preserves 10 and 13 decisions that the defense arm
turns into abstentions; the reverse never occurs), and
the aggregate submission precision is 100\% for both
systems when they do submit.

\paragraph{Interface decomposition.}
The three actor systems separate structural from
semantic failure modes.
Under the strict interface without guidance (Batch A),
22 of 24 defense-arm units die in engineering
failures, and the ordinary arm terminates in two
call-limit exhaustions on misleading charts and two
engineering failures on clean charts.
Introducing a schema-guided proxy that mirrors the
sealed structural rules (Batch B) drives the ordinary
actor's structural failures to zero (12/12 on clean
charts) and cuts defense-arm engineering failures only
modestly (21 of 24), revealing that the binding
constraint is semantic: exact public-leaf values,
bounded summaries, and the question--response count
coupling cannot be expressed by any response format.
Running the frontier model directly through the gateway
(Batch C) removes the structural interface almost
entirely---defense-arm engineering failures fall to 4
of 24---and the remaining mass is honest abstention:
12 \texttt{unresolved\_public\_evidence} verdicts, most
of them on misleading charts, where verification cannot
close the gap between the printed chart and the
question's required scope within the re-verification
budget.

\paragraph{Stability.}
Re-running Batch C under the identical configuration
changes 11 of 48 unit outcomes, every one of them a
flip between a correct submission and an abstention
(agreement 37/48); no wrong submission appears in
either run, so the ``never submit a wrong answer''
property is stable while completion throughput is not.
Token budgets are comparable across the two runs
(1.80M input and 129K output tokens, 322 attempts and
242 browser operations, versus 1.81M and 115K, 331 and
254), confirming that the stateful machinery operates
within a fixed per-unit allowance comparable to the
static panel's per-task cost.
