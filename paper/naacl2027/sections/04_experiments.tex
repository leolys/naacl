This section reports the two evaluation deployments of
\ours{}: a static paired panel that isolates the
verification core, and a stateful online deployment that
adds the persistent rule state inside a live browser
harness.

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:exp:setting}

The task basis is inherited from our companion
benchmark \bench, which pairs 140 executable web
tasks---47 business, 35 environment, 19 health, 39
public-statistics, in 21 mechanism families---with a
faithful chart and a misleading chart whose
presentation violates an encoding convention while the
data remain drawable.\footnote{Companion
submission under review; instance construction,
taxonomy, and scoring are defined there.}
Model inputs never contain gold labels, mechanism
labels, the other chart variant, or scoring feedback.

\emph{Static panel.}
For every task the agent sees the benchmark's
misleading chart---scored against its frozen original
label---plus the public goal and four options, and must
submit a selection; no browser navigation occurs.
Six configurations are compared on identical inputs and
decoding (temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.8, top-$k$ 20,
fixed seed, thinking disabled): direct selection and
five READ--VERIFY--DECIDE configurations
(Section~\ref{sec:method}) with frozen prompts, one
verification round ($L=1$), and three requests per
task, whose candidate chains are the frozen per-task
pool of one to three pre-generated $O/B$ records (275
across the panel).
V3 verifies $O$ and $B$ in distinct schema-typed
passes, V4 fixes explicit numeric references, V5 adds
same-series query-point constraints with scale,
time-window, and policy-boundary checks, V6 checks
necessary conditions item by item, and V7 keeps V4's
prompts with an abstention-aware decision repair
phase; the six configurations and prompts were frozen
before the replication and seed rounds and not
modified after seeing round results.
Interface failures and empty selections remain in the
140 denominator; \emph{robust accuracy} throughout is
the fraction of tasks whose selection matches the
frozen original label, the same full-denominator
quantity for every configuration.
Frozen prompts, typed schemas, decision logs, the
interface contract, and the scoring code are in the
supplementary material, so every number here can be
recomputed from released artifacts once the companion
benchmark's license permits release.

\emph{Online stateful deployment.}
Twelve tasks spanning all four domains, taken in fixed
identifier order, run as live browser sessions under a
closed deployment: each task's two chart variants
execute under both ordinary execution and the \ours{}
arm, whose hooks, rule state, and re-derivation loops
run inside the harness with the actor driving a real
(Playwright-controlled) browser.
The submission interface is a strict typed protocol
(\texttt{typed\_scalars\_v2}; at most eight actor
calls, one re-verification round, temperature 0): a
unit ends as a scored submission, an abstention with a
recorded reason, or a failure.
Three actor systems are used---Qwen3.8-27B under
strict response formats (Batch A), the same model
behind a schema-guided proxy mirroring the sealed
structural rules (Batch B), and the pilot's frontier
model \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra} through an
OpenAI-compatible gateway (Batch C, plus one stability
rerun).

Table~\ref{tab:static-main} reports the original round
under Qwen3.8-27B: direct selection sits at
57.9\% (81/140) and the full configuration V5 at
68.6\% (96/140), a net gain of 15 decision points with
no increase in abstention or interface failure.
Because the panel is entirely single-decision-point,
the gain cannot be attributed to information gained
after selection: verification changes the decision
already available.

Table~\ref{tab:static-seed} reports a
matched-configuration replication separating sampling
variance from configuration effects: direct selection
and V5 are re-executed under three request seeds
(12345, 22345, 32345) on a single-GPU deployment
matching the frozen service except batch admission, so
comparisons stay internal to it
(Appendix~\ref{app:details} records the protocol).
The same run supplies three baselines: an SC3
self-consistency vote over the direct-selection seeds
\citep{wang2022selfconsistency} (majority among
non-null selections, ties as abstentions), a
tabular-answering baseline in the style of Tonglet et
al. \citep{tonglet2025protecting} (bounded transcription
of comparison-relevant rows, then a decision from the
table alone with the image withheld), and a clean-chart
control running direct selection on the paired clean
charts.

The 27B columns of Table~\ref{tab:static-main} report
the single-run replication that keeps model, seeds, and
prompts frozen: direct selection drops from 81 to 73 of
140 under an identical configuration, quantifying the
sampling variance of agentic decoding itself, so
comparisons must be made within the same run.
Within-run, V5 gains $+17$ paired decisions
($p=.0013$; 95\% CI $[+0.061, +0.231]$), V4, V6, and
V7 gain $+13$, $+13$, $+16$ ($p\leq0.0025$ each), and
V3 returns to $+2$ despite strongly directional
wrong--target flips (30 against 6, $p<.001$ on the
caption's discordant-pair test) because its interface
failures absorb the difference.
The replication's paired transfers are one-directional
in aggregate (57 keep, 33 error-to-target, 11
target-to-error, 5 target-to-empty), and the panel
remains abstention-free outside V3's interface
failures; the same directionality holds in every
seed of the matched-configuration replication above,
whose per-seed nets the baselines paragraph reports.

\paragraph{Baselines and controls.}
The clean-chart control quantifies the execution-level
damage of misleading presentations: direct selection
rises from 56.2\% on misleading charts to 82.1\% on the
paired clean charts (paired net $+33$, $p<.001$, exact
CI $[+0.16, +0.27]$).
The two generic baselines do not close that gap: SC3
reaches 53.6\%, indistinguishable from direct selection
(paired net $-3$, $p=.375$)---consistent with
Section~\ref{sec:method:setting}, the object of
correction is the interpretation rule, not the answer
distribution; and the tabular-answering baseline
abstains on 55 of 140 units with 18 transcription
failures, selecting with 59.7\% precision (40/67), a
paired net of $-3$ ($p=.51$).
Across the three seeds, direct selection lands at
$56.2\,\pm\,1.1$ and \ours{} at $65.7\,\pm\,0.7$
accuracy points; the per-seed paired nets $+12$, $+18$,
$+15$ pool to $+45$ decisions over 420 paired units
(exact McNemar $p=1.9\times10^{-5}$, 95\% CI
$[+0.058, +0.150]$).

\paragraph{Cross-model transfer.}
On Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, direct selection drops to
46.4\% and the picture splits
(Table~\ref{tab:static-main}, 8B columns): V7 gains
$+15$ ($p=.021$; CI $[+.017,+.210]$), V5 gains
$+10$ ($p=0.044$), V4 and V6 remain within noise, and
V3 collapses through 51 interface failures despite
directional wrong--target flips
(Appendix~\ref{app:transfer}).
The separated verification prompts that produce
directional flips on the 27B model overwhelm the 8B
actor, so interface robustness is a first-class
variable, and prompt content and interface contract
must be evaluated jointly rather than as one
``method'' factor.

\subsection{Online Stateful Results}\label{sec:exp:online}



A pilot (6 tasks, \texttt{gpt-5.6-terra}) already
showed this pattern (5 correct ordinary selections
against 1 for \ours{}).
Because the deployment's submission protocol is
fail-closed, the primary online metric is the
\emph{wrong-submission rate}---the defense-side
counterpart of agent abstention
\citep{liu2026agentabstain, luo2026agentic}: across
the four batches,
ordinary execution produced six wrong submissions, all
under the 27B actor on misleading charts, while \ours{}
produced none in any batch or chart variant (0 of 96
defense-arm units); Table~\ref{tab:online-four} gives
the full four-batch decomposition.
Aggregate accuracy moves oppositely: ordinary execution
loses 3--5 decision points on misleading charts
relative to its own clean-chart performance, and the
defense arm converts correct ordinary decisions into
recorded abstentions rather than corrections.
The paired effect is strictly one-directional in both
frontier-model batches (10 and 13 preserved decisions
become abstentions; the reverse never occurs; exact
sign tests $p=.002$ and $p<.001$), with submission
precision 100\% (26/26 and 27/27).


\paragraph{Interface decomposition.}
The three actor systems separate structural from
semantic failure modes: under the strict interface
without guidance (Batch A) 20 of 24 defense-arm units
die in engineering failures, while the schema-guided
proxy (Batch B) drives the ordinary actor's structural
failures to zero but cuts defense-arm engineering
failures only modestly, and the direct gateway (Batch
C) leaves honest abstention as the remaining mass
(Appendix~\ref{app:interface}).

A stability rerun of Batch C under the identical
configuration changes 11 of 48 unit outcomes, every one
a flip between a correct submission and an abstention
(agreement 37/48); the ``never submit a wrong answer''
property is stable while completion throughput is not,
with comparable token budgets across the two runs
(Appendix~\ref{app:stability}).
