Misleading charts are a runtime evidence problem: the
agent's own reading becomes part of its state, so a
defense must compete explanations at the moment of
reading and keep revised rules in force.
\ours{} implements this pair through
competing-explanation chains, in-chart verification,
and a persistent rule state revised only through
Keep/Revise/Unresolved decisions.
The static panel shows a robust-accuracy gain that
replicates within-run, across seeds, and on an 8B
agent; interface contracts co-determine outcomes as
strongly as prompt content; and online, under a
fail-closed protocol, the defense arm never submits a
wrong answer; residual mass becomes recorded
abstentions or unrecovered terminations.
We release decision logs, rule states, and
re-derivation traces so future work can measure which
explanation an agent acted on and whether it survived
the chart.
