We treated misleading charts as a runtime evidence
problem for executing web agents rather than a
benchmark artifact: the agent's own reading of a chart
becomes part of its state, so a defense must both
compete explanations at the moment of reading and keep
revised rules in force afterwards.
\ours{} implements this pair of requirements with a
competing-explanation chain over visible chart
evidence, an in-chart verification pass that constrains
query points with same-series references, and a
persistent rule state that is revised only through a
KEEP/REVISE/UNRESOLVED decision with recorded evidence
and scope.
The static panel shows that this machinery yields a
within-run robust-accuracy gain of 10--17 paired
decision points on a 27B open-weight agent, replicates
under a frozen configuration, and transfers to an 8B
agent through the decision-oriented variant, while the
failure analysis shows that interface contracts
co-determine outcomes as strongly as prompt content.
The stateful online deployment shows the complement:
under a fail-closed submission protocol the defense arm
never submits a wrong answer and its residual errors
become legible abstentions with recorded reasons, at a
throughput cost that our interface decomposition
attributes to the semantic contract of the protocol
rather than to the reasoning itself.
We release the full decision logs, rule states, and
re-derivation traces so that future work can measure
not only whether agents answer correctly, but which
explanation they acted on and whether it survived
contact with the chart.
