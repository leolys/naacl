Research on misleading visualizations has developed
along two lines that our defense connects.
The first line studies how charts mislead human readers:
\citet{lisnic2023misleading} show that deception in
charts relies less on outright visual distortion than on
the combination of faithful drawings with selective
context, framing, and commentary, and current
visualization research extends this analysis to
persuasive chart design \citep{nadib2026guardrail}.
The second line measures the same phenomenon in models:
benchmarks evaluate multimodal LLMs on misleading chart
question answering \citep{chen2025unmasking,
bharti2024chartom, alexander2024models},
\citet{mahbub2025perils} and
\citet{mahbub2026chart} quantify how truncated axes,
distorted aspect ratios, and manipulated encodings
degrade chart reasoning across model families and probe
mitigations, and recent studies examine whether agents
and LLMs recognize misleadingness without being nudged
\citep{panda2026making, blasilli2026true, seto2026llms}.
Method-side responses include tool-augmented chart
reasoning that grounds answers in extracted chart
elements \citep{kaur2025chartagent}, structured
extraction pipelines that repair extraction--inference
gaps \citep{das2025chartsofthought}, detection of
misleading charts as a classification task
\citep{tonglet2025this}, and agentic dual-path
frameworks that separate data fidelity checking from
conclusion checking \citep{zhang2026navigating}.
\citet{tonglet2025protecting} provide the closest
model-level defense, protecting multimodal LLMs through
tabular answering and chart re-plotting; these
procedures repair a single question--answer round, while
\ours{} targets the interpretation rules that recur
across the rounds of an executing agent and maintains
their validated revisions in runtime state.

A second related line concerns the integrity of agent
environments.
Indirect prompt injection benchmarks show that content
returned from tools and web pages can steer tool-augmented
agents \citep{zhan2024injecagent}, web-agent security
benchmarks measure targeted hijacking under realistic
threat models \citep{evtimov2025wasp}, and demonstrations
show injection through the very representations agents
perceive, such as HTML accessibility trees
\citep{johnson2025manipulating}; automated red-teaming
generates such attacks at scale \citep{wang2025agentvigil}
and runtime frameworks intercept them
\citep{zhao2026clawguard}.
Defenses at the interface level separate instructions
from data through structured queries \citep{chen2024struq}
or preference-optimized training against injected
content \citep{chen2024secalign}.
\citet{wu2024dissecting} dissect the robustness of
multimodal agents as compound systems and show that
reflection and tree search, the components commonly
added for self-correction, open new vulnerabilities when
the evaluator itself is exposed to attacked content.
Our defense differs in what it protects: not the agent's
instruction hierarchy but the interpretation layer
between visual observation and task judgment, where a
misleading chart exerts its effect without any injected
instruction.

The third line concerns persistent state and correction
in agents.
Verbal reinforcement learning carries textual feedback
across attempts \citep{shinn2023reflexion},
self-consistency aggregates multiple reasoning paths
into one answer \citep{wang2022selfconsistency}, and
intrinsic self-correction without external feedback
degrades reasoning performance
\citep{huang2023large}.
Agent surveys organize the growing design space of
state and memory components
\citep{wang2024survey, hu2025agents}, and
\citet{koh2024visualwebarena} establish that realistic
visual web tasks remain hard enough that correctness of
single decisions cannot be taken for granted.
\ours{} borrows the state-carrying idea from memory
research but changes the carried content: instead of
outcome feedback or free-form reflections, the state
records interpretation rules with their evidential
support, applicability scope, and revision history, so
that later steps inherit what was verified about the
chart rather than a verdict about the answer.
