Research on misleading visualizations has developed
along two lines that our defense connects.
The first line studies how charts mislead human readers:
\citet{lisnic2023misleading} show that deception relies
less on outright visual distortion than on faithful
drawings combined with selective context and framing
\citep{nadib2026guardrail}.
The second measures the same phenomenon in models:
benchmarks evaluate multimodal LLMs on misleading chart
QA \citep{chen2025unmasking, bharti2024chartom,
alexander2024models}; \citet{mahbub2025perils} and
\citet{mahbub2026chart} quantify how truncated axes,
distorted aspect ratios, and manipulated encodings
degrade chart reasoning and probe mitigations; and
recent studies ask whether agents recognize
misleadingness unprompted \citep{panda2026making,
blasilli2026true, seto2026llms}.
Method-side responses include tool-augmented chart
reasoning \citep{kaur2025chartagent}, structured
extraction pipelines \citep{das2025chartsofthought},
misleading-chart detection \citep{tonglet2025this}, and
agentic dual-path frameworks that separate data-fidelity
from conclusion checking \citep{zhang2026navigating}.
\citet{tonglet2025protecting} provide the closest
model-level defense, protecting multimodal LLMs through
tabular answering and chart re-plotting; these
procedures repair a single question--answer round, while
\ours{} targets the interpretation rules that recur
across the rounds of an executing agent and maintains
their validated revisions in runtime state.

A second line concerns agent-environment integrity.
Indirect prompt injection benchmarks show that tool and
web content can steer agents \citep{zhan2024injecagent};
web-agent security benchmarks measure targeted hijacking
\citep{evtimov2025wasp}; injection reaches the very
representations agents perceive, such as HTML
accessibility trees \citep{johnson2025manipulating};
automated red-teaming generates such attacks at scale
\citep{wang2025agentvigil} and runtime frameworks
intercept them \citep{zhao2026clawguard}.
Interface-level defenses separate instructions from data
\citep{chen2024struq} or train against injected content
\citep{chen2024secalign}.
\citet{wu2024dissecting} dissect the robustness of
multimodal agents as compound systems and show that
reflection and tree search, the components commonly
added for self-correction, open new vulnerabilities when
the evaluator itself is exposed to attacked content.
Our defense differs in what it protects: not the agent's
instruction hierarchy but the interpretation layer
between visual observation and task judgment, where a
misleading chart exerts its effect without any injected
instruction.

The third line concerns persistent state and correction.
Verbal reinforcement carries textual feedback across
attempts \citep{shinn2023reflexion}, self-consistency
aggregates reasoning paths \citep{wang2022selfconsistency},
and intrinsic self-correction without external feedback
degrades reasoning \citep{huang2023large,
li2025decomposing}.
Agent surveys organize the design space of state and
memory components \citep{wang2024survey, hu2025agents},
and \citet{koh2024visualwebarena} establish that
realistic visual web tasks remain hard enough that
single-decision correctness cannot be taken for
granted.
\ours{} borrows the state-carrying idea but changes the
carried content: the state records interpretation rules
with evidential support, applicability scope, and
revision history, so later steps inherit what was
verified about the chart rather than a verdict about
the answer.
