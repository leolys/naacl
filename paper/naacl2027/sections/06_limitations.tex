This work studies defensive scaffolding around
vision--language agents; its limitations follow from
the same design choices that produce the results.

\paragraph{Scale and models.}
The static panel uses one 27B and one 8B open-weight
VLM under fixed decoding; the online deployment adds
one frontier model through a gateway.
We do not claim the gains generalize to proprietary or
future models, other agent frameworks, or tasks outside
the four covered domains; the 8B transfer already shows that variant choice interacts with actor capability in ways we do not fully predict.

\paragraph{Baseline coverage.}
The panel compares against self-consistency, bounded
tabular answering, and a clean-chart control; a
prompt-instruction hardening control (instructing the
actor to re-check axis labels) was not run and would
test a complementary, model-side mitigation.

\paragraph{Measurement and variance.}
Robust accuracy is computed against the original option
labels, and agreement checks verify that submitted
options and rule states match the recorded logs; label
agreement is not semantic verification, a run may rest on a misreading the frozen labels do not separate, recent benchmark-validity critiques \citep{lan2026seeing} caution against reading any such score as grounded visual understanding, and the rule states we analyze are the agent's own declared rules.
The paired gains are within-run comparisons on a fixed
task set, with sampling variance quantified three ways:
the drop of direct selection from 81 to 73 of 140
between the two frozen rounds, the three-seed spread of
$\pm1.1$ accuracy points around 56.2\%, and McNemar
tests with exact confidence intervals on paired
decisions rather than cross-run rate differences;
engine-level changes such as batch admission also shift
individual-seed outcomes slightly, so cross-engine
comparisons are avoided.
The 24-task development subset was used to calibrate
configuration emphasis and is scored separately
throughout; still, all configurations were designed by
the authors after inspecting aggregate failure
patterns, so the panel should be read as a controlled
comparison rather than a held-out evaluation.

\paragraph{Interface dependence and runtime.}
The online results are conditional on the strict
submission protocol, whose semantic contract---exact
public-leaf values, bounded summaries, count-coupled
responses---cannot be expressed in a response format;
under earlier actor systems the same machinery died in
engineering failures rather than abstentions, so the
deployment demonstrates \ours{} under one operating
point, not an interface-robust defense, and the negative environment-domain results and one-directional online paired effect are reported as found. The V3--V7 ladder varies verification content and interface prompts jointly, so the 8B attribution to interface robustness is argued from the failure-mode decomposition, not isolated by a prompt--interface cross ablation.
Serving the six static configurations takes roughly
$2.6\times$ as many requests as there are decisions
(2,230 requests for 840 decisions in the 27B rerun
ledger), and the online arm
operates within a fixed per-unit budget (one
re-verification round, eight actor calls); agents under
tighter latency or cost envelopes may not accommodate
this overhead.

\paragraph{State integrity.}
The rule state is trusted harness memory: the isolation
that keeps environment content from writing rules
directly is assumed, and persistent-state poisoning attacks on agent memory \citep{louck2026securing}---and, more broadly, injection--execution dissociation \citep{leong2026injectionexecution}---fall outside the present threat model and remain targets for future work.

\paragraph{Ethics and reproducibility boundary.}
The defense direction of this work is protective, but
the same observation---that faithful chart pixels admit
misleading encodings that change executed
actions---can inform adversarial chart design, so
instances are released only through the benchmark's own
access process and no new manipulation techniques are
published.
The companion benchmark is under review and not yet
public; until release the results cannot be
independently recomputed from the logs alone, although
the decision logs, prompts, and rule states of the
original, replication, 8B, and online rounds are
preserved verbatim in the supplementary material, and
the three-seed replication survives as summary
statistics and scoring outputs.
