This work studies defensive scaffolding around
vision--language agents, and its limitations follow
from the same design choices that produce the results.

\paragraph{Scale and models.}
The static panel uses one 27B open-weight VLM and one
8B open-weight VLM under fixed decoding; the online
deployment uses the same 27B model under two interface
regimes and one frontier model accessed through a
gateway.
We do not claim that the reported gains generalize to
proprietary or future models, to other agent
frameworks, or to tasks outside the four covered
domains; the 8B transfer already shows that variant
choice interacts with actor capability in ways we do
not fully predict.

\paragraph{What the labels measure.}
Robust accuracy is computed against the original
option labels of the benchmark, and our agreement
checks verify that submitted options and rule states
match the recorded logs.
Label agreement is not semantic verification: a run
whose recorded reasoning matches its outcome may still
rest on a misreading that the frozen labels do not
separate, and conversely a correct submission may be
produced for partially wrong reasons.
The rule states we analyze are the agent's own
declared rules, not independently validated facts.

\paragraph{Selection effects and variance.}
The paired gains are within-run comparisons on a fixed
task set; the drop of direct selection from 81 to 73
of 140 under an identical configuration quantifies
sampling variance, and we therefore report McNemar
tests and exact confidence intervals on paired
decisions rather than cross-run rate differences.
The 24-task development subset was used to calibrate
configuration emphasis and is scored separately
throughout; still, all configurations were designed by
the authors after inspecting aggregate failure
patterns, so the panel should be read as a controlled
comparison rather than a held-out evaluation.

\paragraph{Interface dependence.}
The online results are conditional on the strict
submission protocol.
Its semantic contract---exact public-leaf values,
bounded summaries, count-coupled responses---cannot be
expressed in a response format, and under earlier
actor systems the same machinery died in engineering
failures rather than abstentions.
The deployment therefore demonstrates the behavior of
\ours{} under one operating point (frontier actor,
direct gateway, strict typed interface), not a
interface-robust defense; the negative environment-domain
results of the static panel and the one-directional
online paired effect are reported as found.

\paragraph{Runtime footprint.}
Verification multiplies requests by roughly
$2.6\times$ over direct selection and the online
arm operates within a fixed per-unit budget
(1 re-verification round, 8 actor calls); agents
deployed under tighter latency or cost envelopes may
not accommodate this overhead.

\paragraph{Ethics and dual use.}
The defense direction of this work is protective, but
the same observation---that faithful chart pixels admit
misleading encodings that change executed
actions---can inform adversarial chart design; we
therefore release instances only through the
benchmark's own access process and avoid publishing
new manipulation techniques.

\paragraph{Reproducibility boundary.}
The companion benchmark is under review and its
instances are not yet public; until release, the
results here cannot be independently recomputed from
the logs alone, although all decision logs, prompts,
and rule states referenced in this paper are preserved
verbatim in the submission's supplementary material.
